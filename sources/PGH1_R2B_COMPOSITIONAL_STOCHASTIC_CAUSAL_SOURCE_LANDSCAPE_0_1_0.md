## SG4A — Markov categories and synthetic probability

Giry supplies the foundational categorical probability ancestry through probability measures/kernels and monadic structure.

Jacobs' distribution-monad/convex-category work develops explicit structural relations between probability scalars, monads, and categorical convexity.

Fritz's 2020 Markov-category paper is the central modern mechanism source: conditioning, disintegration, conditional independence, sufficient statistics, and classical statistical theorems can be formulated in abstract categorical terms that apply uniformly across discrete, measure-theoretic, Gaussian, and stochastic-process settings.

Fritz and Liang sharpen an especially PGH-relevant distinction. A Markov category differs from a gs-monoidal category by terminality of the monoidal unit, corresponding to normalization. Thus a doctrine-level structural axiom can exclude non-normalized process behavior without enumerating stochastic matrices. At the same time, free Markov categories are generated from an explicit monoidal signature of boxes. Those boxes remain input data.

Representable Markov categories further show that nontrivial statistical comparison theorems can be formulated abstractly across probability models.

Source-level conclusion:

```text
PROBABILISTIC_STRUCTURE_CAN_HAVE_MODEL_CLASS_CONSEQUENCES = YES
MARKOV_CATEGORY_AXIOMS_FIX_LOCAL_KERNELS = NO
FREE_SIGNATURE_BOXES_REMAIN_INPUT = YES
```

This is a positive mechanism for PGH to test, not a completed law-exhaustion route.

## SG4B — causal theories and compositional Bayesian networks

Fong's causal-theory construction makes the syntax/semantics separation unusually explicit: a causal/compositional category supplies the wiring language, while a strong symmetric monoidal functor into stochastic maps supplies the concrete model.

Fritz's correlation-scenario and arbitrary-causal-structure papers demonstrate that source independence and DAG structure can restrict admissible correlations without selecting one numerical response table.

Henson, Lal and Pusey supply the strongest theory-independence control in this lane. In generalized Bayesian networks, causal structure enforces observable conditional independences across broad generalized probabilistic theories, and additional probability constraints can arise beyond those independences.

Yin and Zhang construct causal syntax through free Markov categories on DAGs and derive causal reasoning/do-calculus structure abstractly.

Fritz and Klingler prove a categorical d-separation theorem applying beyond ordinary discrete probability, including continuous/mixed probability as well as deterministic and possibilistic networks.

Source-level conclusion:

```text
CAUSAL_WIRING_CAN_RESTRICT_MODEL_CLASS = YES
CAUSAL_GRAPH_OR_DAG_IS_ITSELF_INPUT = YES
LOCAL_MECHANISMS_OR_CHANNELS_REMAIN_MODEL_DATA = GENERALLY_YES
```

For PGH this is crucial: causal syntax can produce nontrivial exclusions, but credit depends on whether the causal/compositional structure is part of the preregistered grammar rather than imported from target physics.

## SG4C — conditional independence, disintegration and normalization

Simpson shows that independence and conditional independence have common categorical structure extending beyond probability.

Cho and Jacobs formulate disintegration and Bayesian inversion graphically and abstractly, clarifying which conditional constructions depend on suitable existence conditions.

Fritz develops conditional independence and sufficient-statistic theory inside Markov categories, while Fritz and Klingler connect causal compatibility to categorical d-separation.

The combined lesson is that relations usually expressed through numerical factorizations can often be recognized through structural diagrams and axioms.

But there is no source-level warrant to infer that every relevant conditional distribution is thereby fixed.

That is scientifically more interesting for PGH than merely replacing a probability table by categorical notation.

The answer is already yes in various senses.

## Strongest positive mechanisms identified by the landscape

At source-landscape scope, the clearest candidate mechanisms are:

### M1 — normalization by affine/terminal structure

Markov-category structure can encode normalization categorically.

This is compact, non-table-driven, and applies across a model class.

However, normalization by itself does not select a response distribution among normalized distributions. It therefore cannot alone repair the current R2B failure.

### M2 — causal/DAG factorization and conditional independence

Causal wiring can impose factorization/conditional-independence structure independent of local numerical kernels.

This can remove response models from a class without fixing every local channel.

The remaining PGH burden is the origin and physical relevance of the causal/context transformation structure.

### M3 — model-independent causal-network correlation constraints

Generalized causal networks can impose constraints that survive broad variation of the underlying probabilistic theory.

This is the strongest source-level demonstration that structural causal architecture can have response-level exclusion power across model classes.

The network topology/resources remain substantive inputs.

### M4 — abstract conditional/disintegration calculus

Categorical probability can make conditioning, independence and statistical comparison principles structural across multiple concrete probability theories.

These operations may become useful if a future PGH bridge grammar already supplies independently justified compositional relations among contexts.
