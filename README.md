## Purpose

The **Physical Grammar Hypothesis (PGH)** asks whether the deepest laws of physical possibility can be understood as a compact, representation-independent generative grammar whose well-formed structures or derivations correspond, modulo physical equivalence, to physically possible structures.

PGH is a research hypothesis under construction, not an accepted physical theory.

The live research bottleneck is therefore:

\[
\text{representation equivalence}
\Longrightarrow
\text{coherence}
\Longrightarrow
\text{formal exclusion}.
\]

The unresolved question is whether the first implication can be earned without inserting the desired law into the equivalence relation itself.

## Epistemic rules

1. **No smuggling.** A known physical or algebraic law cannot be renamed as grammar and counted as derived.
2. **Representation invariance.** Deep structure must not depend on arbitrary notation or encoding.
3. **Nontrivial exclusion.** A useful grammar must derive restrictions rather than merely list them.
4. **Generative compression.** Compact structure should do explanatory work rather than store a table.
5. **Failure conditions first.** Countermodels and failed derivations are preserved.
6. **Formal resemblance is not physical derivation.**
7. **No premature FCP import.**
