# PGH-1 Primitive Grammar Admissibility Standard 0.1.0

## Status

```text
OBJECT_ID = PGH-OBJ-0020
OBJECT_CLASS = CANDIDATE_GRAMMAR_ADMISSIBILITY_STANDARD
PHYSICAL_STATUS = NONE
```

## Purpose

Define when a declared primitive generative structure may enter PGH as a scientific **candidate grammar** without requiring a meta-derivation of every primitive.

Candidate admission is not physical confirmation.

## Hard candidate criteria

### P0 — formal definability

The grammar must be specified precisely enough that well-formedness and derivability are independently checkable.

### P1 — primitive disclosure

Every primitive object, operation, relation, type, structural permission, equivalence, semantic input, and background doctrine must be declared in assumption ancestry.

### P2 — pre-target fixation

The candidate must be frozen before evaluating the physical law, phenomenon, or exclusion it is later asked to recover.

### P3 — restricted hypothesis class

The candidate family must not permit arbitrary target theories to be recovered merely by changing an unrestricted table, predicate interpretation, rule list, or equivalent parameterization.

### P4 — no extensional target table

A direct listing or one-symbol naming of the desired admissibility set is not generative explanation.

### P5 — generative compression

A comparatively compact structural package must generate a materially larger or richer consequence space by rule reuse.

No exact universal complexity metric is assumed at version 0.1.0. The compression claim must nevertheless be explicit and auditable.

### P6 — nontrivial exclusion

At least one mathematically specifiable structure must be excluded for a reason not definitionally equivalent to the desired target conclusion.

### P7 — representation discipline

The candidate must either pass a defined representation-invariance test or preregister the transformations under which its claimed deep structure is expected to survive.

### P8 — law-free semantic boundary

The fixed empirical-reference anchor may not carry the response law, probability rule, complete possibility set, or target physical partition.

### P9 — independent consequence

At least one consequence must follow from the declared grammar rather than from an independently inserted law or semantic selector.

### P10 — counterexample exposure

The candidate must specify mathematical or later physical conditions that could falsify, block, or downgrade it.

### P11 — separate physical bridge

Formal candidacy does not satisfy the physical bridge. A later operation must establish why grammar-derived restrictions correspond to physical restrictions.

## Blocking failures

Any of the following blocks candidate admission:

```text
UNRESTRICTED_UNIVERSAL_ENCODING
TARGET_RESULT_USED_TO_CHOOSE_RULES
UNDECLARED_SUBSTANTIVE_ASSUMPTION
SEMANTIC_RESPONSE_LAW_SMUGGLING
NO_NONTRIVIAL_EXCLUSION
NO_COUNTEREXAMPLE_CONDITION
```

## Candidate statuses

A candidate may receive only descriptive statuses:

```text
BLOCKED_ENCODING
FORMALLY_DEFINED_BUT_NONSELECTIVE
NONTRIVIAL_FORMAL_CANDIDATE
PHYSICAL_BRIDGE_UNESTABLISHED
PHYSICALLY_TESTABLE_CANDIDATE
REFUTED_CANDIDATE
```

No scalar score and no framework winner may be inferred from this standard.

## Multiple-candidate policy

Uniqueness is not required at candidate-admission scope.

Competing primitive grammars may be preregistered under the same standard and compared only after their assumptions are frozen.

## Relationship to PGH

The standard permits primitive grammar postulates because PGH hypothesizes a grammar `G`; it does not license an independent substantive law set outside `G`.

The explanatory claim survives only if the grammar's compact primitive structure generates the substantive restrictions for which PGH claims credit.
