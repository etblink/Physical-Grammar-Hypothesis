# PGH-DER-0021 — Free Anchor Atomic Cross-Type Nonselection

## Claim

Under the common law-free seed with distinct atomic context generators `X_c`, record generators `Y_r`, and no nonstructural morphism generators, none of the frozen primitive grammar candidates forces a morphism

\[
X_c\to Y_r.
\]

## Bare monoidal case

The free strict monoidal category has only identity arrows on identical tensor words. Distinct atomic words admit no arrow.

## Symmetric monoidal case

Structural arrows generated by symmetry and monoidal coherence preserve atomic generator multiplicities. They may permute factors but cannot change a context atom into a distinct record atom.

## Cartesian case

Assume for contradiction that the free cartesian category contains `f:X_c->Y_r`.

Assign generators into `Set` by

\[
X_c\mapsto 1,
\qquad
Y_r\mapsto\varnothing.
\]

The universal property extends the assignment to a finite-product-preserving interpretation. The image of `f` would be a function `1->empty`, impossible.

Hence no `f` exists.

## Cocartesian case

Use the same separating assignment into `Set`, now as a finite-coproduct category. A forced arrow again yields the impossible function `1->empty`.

## Bicartesian case

`Set` has finite products and coproducts. The same interpretation separates the atomic generators and again rules out a doctrine-forced `X_c->Y_r`.

## Consolidated result

For every current candidate:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{F_i}(X_c,Y_r)=\varnothing
\]

for distinct context and record atoms.

Therefore:

```text
CURRENT_DOCTRINE_PLUS_LAW_FREE_LABELS =>/=> CROSS_TYPE_EMPIRICAL_CONNECTIVITY
```
