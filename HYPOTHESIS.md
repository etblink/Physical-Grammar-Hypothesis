# HYPOTHESIS

## 1. Purpose

This file records the current hypothesis family under investigation. None of the forms below is established as true.

## 2. Weak Physical Grammar Hypothesis

### PGH-W

> Physical theories admit useful description in terms of generative, compositional, contextual, or grammatical structure.

This claim is intentionally weak and may be too broad to be scientifically interesting. Many formal systems satisfy something like it.

Current status:

```text
PGH_W = PLAUSIBLE_BUT_POTENTIALLY_TRIVIAL
```

## 3. Strong Physical Grammar Hypothesis

### PGH-S

> There exists a compact, representation-independent generative grammar `G` such that the physically possible structures of the universe correspond, up to physical equivalence, to the well-formed structures or derivations generated by `G`, and the substantive laws of physical possibility arise from the grammar rather than from an additional independently imposed law set.

Schematic form:

\[
P \cong W(G)/{\sim}.
\]

Here:

- `G` is not yet formally fixed;
- `W(G)` denotes well-formed grammatical structures or derivations;
- `~` denotes an eventual notion of physical equivalence;
- `P` denotes an eventual notion of physical possibility.

Current status:

```text
PGH_S = OPEN_UNESTABLISHED
```

## 4. Linguistic–Physical Identity Hypothesis

### PGH-I

A still stronger speculative form is:

> The deep principles governing meaningful generative structure and the deep principles governing physical possibility are realizations of one underlying constraint architecture.

This does **not** currently assert that English syntax, human cognition, or any empirical natural-language grammar is literally a law of physics.

It leaves open whether human linguistic structure could later be shown to instantiate some deeper grammar also realized physically.

Current status:

```text
PGH_I = SPECULATIVE_NOT_OPERATIONALIZED
```

## 5. Null hypothesis

### PGH-H0

> Grammatical structure belongs to formal representation, modeling practice, cognition, or mathematical language, while independent physical laws remain necessary to select physical possibilities.

Schematic form:

\[
P = \mathrm{Select}_{L}(W(G)),
\]

where an independent physical law set `L` performs the substantive selection.

If the physics remains in `L`, PGH-S has not succeeded.

Current status:

```text
PGH_H0 = LIVE_NULL
```

## 6. Candidate grammar scaffold

A useful future scaffold may take a form such as

\[
G=(T,C,\circ,\equiv,D),
\]

with:

- `T`: types, distinctions, or admissible terms;
- `C`: contextual or well-formedness constraints;
- `∘`: partial composition;
- `≡`: equivalence or identification;
- `D`: permitted derivations or transformations.

This tuple is **not** an adopted fundamental ontology. It is bookkeeping for questions that a precise grammar may need to answer.

The current project is actively testing whether some of these ingredients can be derived from the smaller candidate primitive set:

```text
DISTINGUISH
COMPOSE
IDENTIFY
```

## 7. Required properties of a successful strong PGH

### 7.1 Generativity

The grammar must produce a rich space of admissible structures from a comparatively compact rule set.

A mere list of physically allowed histories does not qualify.

### 7.2 Exclusion

Some mathematically describable candidate structures must be ungrammatical.

Otherwise the grammar performs no physical selection.

### 7.3 Representation invariance

The deep content of the grammar must survive faithful changes of surface mathematical language.

If an allegedly fundamental grammatical rule vanishes under an equivalent reformulation, it is likely representational rather than physical.

### 7.4 No physical smuggling

Substantive physics must not be hidden in:

- primitive definitions;
- production rules;
- typing rules;
- admissibility conditions;
- boundary conditions;
- semantics;
- the interpretation map;
- an unexplained choice of initial structure.

### 7.5 Generative compression

The explanatory complexity of the grammar should be materially smaller than the complexity of the structures it accounts for.

At PGH-0 no formal complexity measure is fixed.

### 7.6 Independent consequence

At least one substantive restriction must follow from the grammar that was not independently inserted as a physical law.

Without this, PGH-S remains a redescription.

### 7.7 Failure exposure

A successful candidate must make claims specific enough that a mathematical counterexample, equivalent reformulation, or physical observation could count against it.

## 8. Distinguishing grammatical necessity from physical necessity

The project must keep separate:

\[
\text{necessary given a representation}
\]

from

\[
\text{necessary for physical possibility}.
\]

PGH-S proposes that some representation-independent grammatical necessity may be physically constitutive.

It does **not** infer physicality from formal genericity alone.

## 9. Translation-invariance conjecture

A central candidate criterion is:

> A plausible deep physical grammar should be recoverable as invariant structure across faithful formulations that use genuinely different mathematical surface languages.

Schematically, for surface languages `L1`, `L2`, and `L3`:

\[
L_1 \to G,\qquad L_2 \to G,\qquad L_3 \to G.
\]

This is currently a methodological conjecture, not a theorem.

No definition of “faithful translation” has yet been fixed.

## 10. Potential falsifiers or downgrade conditions

PGH-S should be downgraded if any of the following becomes unavoidable:

### F1 — Universal encoding only

Every candidate grammar can encode arbitrary physical law sets with equal ease and no independent structural preference.

### F2 — Physics hidden in semantics

The semantic interpretation map must already contain the substantive dynamics or selection rules.

### F3 — No exclusion

The grammar cannot rule out any otherwise describable possibility without adding physical assumptions.

### F4 — Representation dependence

Supposedly fundamental grammatical structure changes essentially under faithful equivalent reformulation.

### F5 — No compression

The grammar is as complex as the physical histories it reproduces.

### F6 — Failed physical prohibition

A concrete grammar entails that a physically observed phenomenon is impossible.

This falsifies that candidate grammar and may count against broader PGH depending on how much auxiliary freedom remains.

### F7 — Irreducible independent law set

A separate physical law set `L` remains necessary after the grammar is fully specified, with `L` doing the substantive selection work.

### F8 — Irreducible vagueness

“Grammar” cannot be sharpened beyond the generic statement that the universe obeys rules.

## 12. Current research burden

The immediate burden is not to defend PGH-S philosophically.

It is to determine whether a minimal candidate grammar can pass the nontriviality tests in `NONTRIVIALITY_TESTS.md` before any known physical structure is inserted.
