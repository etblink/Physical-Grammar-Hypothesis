This charter establishes a lightweight but rigorous research record for the Physical Grammar Hypothesis (PGH).

The repository exists to preserve conceptual progress, failed derivations, definitions, tests, and handoffs without depending on any single chat context window.

It does **not** establish PGH as a physical theory, an FCP framework, or an empirically supported proposition.

## 2. Scientific target

PGH investigates whether physical possibility may be generated by a compact, representation-independent grammar rather than by a distinct set of dynamical laws imposed on otherwise unconstrained structures.

The strong target may be written schematically as

\[
P \cong W(G)/{\sim},
\]

where `G` is a candidate grammar, `W(G)` its well-formed structures or derivations, `~` physical equivalence, and `P` physical possibility.

The project is successful only if that statement becomes substantively stronger than the trivial observation that any lawful system can be encoded in a formal language.

## 3. Opening research question

The PGH-0 research question is:

> Can an extremely weak grammar, provisionally organized around **DISTINGUISH, COMPOSE, IDENTIFY**, imply any nontrivial constraint on physical possibility without importing known physical content into its primitives, rules, semantics, or interpretation map?

A negative result is admissible and must be preserved.

## 4. Meaning of “grammar” at project opening

“Grammar” initially means an abstract generative architecture involving some combination of:

- well-formedness;
- distinction;
- composition;
- contextual admissibility;
- equivalence;
- transformation or derivation;
- generativity;
- semantic interpretation.

The term does **not** initially mean the contingent grammar of English or any other human natural language.

No identity between natural-language syntax and physical law is assumed.

## 5. Epistemic rules

### 5.1 Truth-seeking rule

The project exists to determine whether PGH is coherent, nontrivial, and physically informative — not to preserve PGH.

### 5.2 No-smuggling rule

A known physical law cannot become explanatory merely by being renamed a grammar rule.

If a candidate rule already contains locality, causality, geometry, probability, quantum structure, a field equation, a conservation law, an action principle, or another substantive physical restriction, that imported content must be identified explicitly.

### 5.3 Trivial-encoding firewall

The existence of a formal encoding of a physical theory is not evidence for PGH.

A grammar that generates exactly the desired physics only because the desired physics was inserted into its production rules fails the explanatory target.

### 5.4 Exclusion requirement

A nontrivial candidate grammar must prohibit at least some otherwise describable structures.

If every mathematically specifiable history is grammatical, the grammar has no physical-selection content.

### 5.5 Representation-invariance requirement

Candidate deep structure must survive faithful changes of mathematical surface representation.

A feature that exists only because one formulation uses a particular notation, object type, coordinate system, or formalism is not yet a deep grammatical invariant.

### 5.6 Generative-compression requirement

A successful grammar should be materially simpler than the physical possibility space it generates.

No exact complexity metric is assumed at PGH-0, but explanatory compression is required in principle.

### 5.7 Semantic-load audit

The map from grammatical structure to physical interpretation must be audited for hidden physical content.

If the semantics map carries the substantive laws that the grammar is claimed to explain, the derivation fails the no-smuggling rule.

### 5.8 Failure-conditions-first rule

Every substantive candidate principle should be accompanied by a statement of what would make it fail, become trivial, or require downgrade.

### 5.9 Failed derivations are first-class results

```text
FAILED_DERIVATIONS_ARE_FIRST_CLASS_RESULTS = YES
```

Failed, circular, representation-dependent, or smuggled derivations must remain in the research record.

They may be superseded, but not rewritten as if they never occurred.

### 5.10 Formal resemblance is not physical derivation

Resemblance to gauge invariance, conservation, symmetry, locality, causal ordering, relational ontology, quantum process theory, category theory, or any other established structure is not evidence that PGH has derived that structure.

A derivation must state its assumptions and show what follows from them.

## 6. FCP quarantine boundary

PGH arose partly from reflection on the distinction between mathematical representation and physical content, but this repository is scientifically independent of FCP.

Require at project opening:


## 7. Source policy

PGH-0 begins as a conceptual formulation and is explicitly:

```text
SOURCE_BOUND_STATUS = NOT_YET_SOURCE_BOUND
```

External literature may later become necessary to distinguish PGH from existing ideas or to formalize candidate structures.

When source work begins, the project must distinguish:

- inspiration;
- prior art;
- mathematical authority;
- physical authority;
- empirical authority;
- independent project derivation.

No future source may be represented as supporting a PGH claim more strongly than it actually does.

## 8. Provenance discipline

`main` is the current accepted project record.

Substantive conceptual changes should be committed with messages that describe the scientific change rather than merely the file operation.

The repository should preserve:

- current state;
- definitions;
- assumptions;
- open questions;
- failed derivations;
- supersession relationships;
- next recommended test;
- handoff capsules sufficient to resume work after context loss.

Exploratory branches may be used later when a bounded derivation or formalization should be isolated before acceptance.

No elaborate governance machinery is required unless the project grows enough to need it.

## 9. Project phases

The following are research stages, not guaranteed milestones.

### PGH-0 — Conceptual formulation

Define the hypothesis, anti-triviality requirements, candidate primitives, and failure conditions.

### PGH-1 — Minimal primitives

Determine whether the proposed primitive set can be reduced and define the surviving primitives precisely.

### PGH-2 — Formal grammar

Specify a mathematically explicit grammar without importing substantive physics.

### PGH-3 — Nontrivial derivations

Attempt to derive restrictions that are not tautological consequences of the definitions and were not inserted by hand.

### PGH-4 — Representation-invariance tests

Test whether candidate deep structures survive faithful reformulations in genuinely different mathematical languages.

### PGH-5 — Physical/falsifiable consequences

Determine whether any surviving grammatical restriction makes contact with physical or empirical possibility in a way that could fail.

Phase progression requires an explicit state update. It is never automatic.

## 11. Opening failure conditions

The strong PGH should be downgraded or rejected if, after reasonable formalization attempts, one or more of the following persists:

1. every candidate grammar is only a redescription of independently supplied physical laws;
2. no nontrivial exclusions arise without importing physical content;
3. the substantive selection resides in the semantics map rather than the grammar;
4. candidate “deep grammar” changes arbitrarily with equivalent mathematical representation;
5. the grammar has no generative compression relative to what it encodes;
6. a concrete grammatical prohibition is contradicted by established physical observation or experiment;
7. the hypothesis cannot be made more precise than “the universe has rules.”

Failure of one candidate grammar does not automatically refute every conceivable PGH, but repeated failures must count against the research program rather than being ignored.
