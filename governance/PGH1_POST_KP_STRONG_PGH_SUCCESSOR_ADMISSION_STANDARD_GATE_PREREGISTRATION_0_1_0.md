## Core admission principle

No future candidate receives empirical strong-PGH status from a grammar alone.

Before empirical target discovery, a candidate must freeze enough structure that later failure cannot be neutralized by changing what the candidate meant, where it applied, how it mapped to observables, or how its statistical implementation was realized.

## Required principles to test

### P1 — complete empirical candidate identity

A future empirical candidate should be treated as

```text
C = (G,J,S,I)
```

before target discovery.

If any component is generated from another, that derivation must be frozen as part of candidate identity.

### P2 — grammar selectivity

`G` must remain non-extensional, non-table-driven, non-universal as an empirical response class, compressive/exclusionary in the relevant formal sense, representation-disciplined, and countermodel/failure exposing.

Universal encoders or complete model classes may serve only as controls, not as candidate explanations.

### P3 — semantic firewall

`J` must map the entire admitted formal model class into a physical/empirical interpretation without choosing favorable parameter values, kernels, model subsets, target-specific equations, or outcomes.

### P4 — scope-origin firewall

`S` must be explicit before target discovery.

Allowed strong-PGH forms are provisionally:

```text
S = TRUE
or
S = RESTRICTED_WITH_PRIOR_F1_F2_OR_TERMINATING_F4_QUALIFICATION
```

Independent F3 regime physics may define a scientifically legitimate weaker/effective candidate but cannot be silently credited to strong PGH unless internalized.

### P5 — prospective instantiation

`I` must determine how eligible real systems/records are mapped to candidate roles without inspecting the candidate's predicted relation, fit, residual, target statistic, or favorable behavior.

If multiple eligible targets exist, selection and tie-breaking must be outcome-neutral and frozen.

### P6 — known-result quarantine

The Kp result is now historical knowledge.

A successor may be killed by an already-known incompatibility with Kp, but compatibility with or accommodation of Kp cannot provide prospective confirmation.

The first positive empirical credit for a successor must come from a target whose relevant behavior was not used in successor construction or selection.

### P7 — revision identity

After empirical exposure, any substantive change to `G`, `J`, restricted `S`, or `I` creates a new/revised candidate identity unless the change was prospectively parameterized by a frozen rule.

A failed target cannot be removed from the old candidate by later scope narrowing.

### P8 — statistical and implementation reproducibility

Before empirical data materialization for any stochastic analysis, the experiment-specific preregistration must freeze at least:

```text
STATISTIC_AND_VERDICT_RULE
MISSINGNESS_AND_SUPPORT_RULES
NULL_GENERATOR
RESAMPLING_COUNTS
PRNG_ALGORITHM
RANDOM_WORD_TO_BOUNDED_INTEGER_MAPPING
PERMUTATION_ALGORITHM
CATEGORICAL_SAMPLER_IF_USED
QUANTILE_AND_P_VALUE_CONVENTIONS
SOFTWARE_AND_VERSION_ASSUMPTIONS
DETERMINISTIC_SEED_DERIVATION
DATA_CUSTODY_AND_HASH_PROCEDURE
```

### P9 — strong versus weaker PGH labeling

A candidate that depends on independent substantive physical-sector rules may still be scientifically interesting, but it must be labeled as weaker/effective/conditional PGH rather than receiving strong-PGH law-exhaustion credit by default.
