## Purpose

PGH has reached formal concepts with obvious potential overlap with established mathematics and foundational-physics methodology. Before proposing another internal invariant-selection axiom, this operation maps the relevant literature and freezes a source landscape capable of answering:

1. Which parts of the current PGH formal machinery are standard or closely related to established mathematics?
2. Which existing theories address equivalence of presentations, coherence, quotienting, rewriting, variable-arity composition, or structural invariance?
3. Which philosophy/foundations literature addresses equivalence between mathematical formulations of physical theories?
4. Which nearby research programs make claims materially similar to strong PGH, and where do they differ?
5. What novelty, if any, remains in PGH after these relationships are made explicit?

This is a source-landscape operation, not a PGH validation exercise.

## Hard boundaries

This operation may:

- search external scholarly and authoritative sources;
- identify primary papers, standard monographs, surveys, and authoritative reference works;
- classify sources by relevance lane;
- reject weak, redundant, or merely terminological matches;
- record likely prior-art overlap and novelty risk;
- freeze a representative source landscape for later adjudication.

It may not:

- alter `PGH-GRAM-0002`;
- claim PGH is novel;
- claim PGH is already present in the literature;
- choose a physical grammar;
- start a physical or empirical bridge;
- import a source result as a PGH axiom without later adjudication;
- change FCP;
- treat source frequency as evidence of physical truth.

## Search lanes

The search must cover at least the following lanes.

### L1 — Universal algebra and term/presentation quotients

Target concepts:

- term algebras;
- congruence relations;
- equations and varieties;
- free algebras;
- presentations by generators and relations;
- factorization through quotients.

### L2 — Term rewriting and confluence

Target concepts:

- rewrite systems;
- normal forms;
- Church–Rosser/confluence;
- completion;
- equational reasoning;
- coherence via rewrites.

### L3 — Categorical coherence

Target concepts:

- Mac Lane coherence;
- monoidal categories;
- associators/unitors;
- strictification;
- coherence theorems;
- distinction between equality and coherent equivalence.

### L4 — Operads, multicategories, and variable-arity composition

Target concepts:

- operations of variable arity;
- substitution/composition;
- trees as presentations;
- symmetric versus nonsymmetric structure;
- quotienting presentation trees.

### L5 — Lawvere theories, monads, and algebraic theories

Target concepts:

- syntax/semantics of algebraic operations;
- presentation-independent algebraic structure;
- free constructions and interpretation maps.

### L6 — String diagrams and graphical calculi

Target concepts:

- representation-independent reasoning by diagrams;
- coherence of graphical presentations;
- topological or combinatorial equivalence of diagrams;
- process composition.

### L7 — Theoretical equivalence and duality in physics

Target concepts:

- when two formulations count as the same theory;
- categorical equivalence;
- definitional/Morita equivalence;
- duality;
- excess structure;
- representational redundancy.

### L8 — Structural realism and relational foundations

Target concepts:

- structural realism;
- relation-first ontology;
- invariants under representation change;
- physical significance of structure versus presentation.

### L9 — Categorical/process approaches to physics

Target concepts:

- process theories;
- categorical quantum mechanics;
- compositionality as foundational architecture;
- operational/category-theoretic representation independence.

No source from this lane may be treated as evidence that PGH is physically correct merely because its language resembles PGH.

### L10 — Formal language, grammar, and compositional generation

Target concepts:

- generative grammar in the abstract mathematical sense;
- formal languages;
- grammars as generators of well-formed structures;
- syntax/semantics separation;
- compositional interpretation.

Human-language-specific material should be admitted only if it bears directly on the strong linguistic–physical identity conjecture or on a formal mechanism used by PGH.

### L11 — Mathematical structuralism / invariance / identity criteria

Target concepts:

- identity up to isomorphism;
- structuralism;
- invariance criteria;
- quotienting redundant presentation structure;
- univalence or related identity/equivalence ideas only where directly relevant.

### L12 — Nearby foundational hypotheses

Search specifically for research programs that might already make claims close to:

```text
PHYSICAL_LAWS_AS_CONSISTENCY_OR_COHERENCE_CONDITIONS
PHYSICAL_POSSIBILITY_AS_WELL_FORMEDNESS
PHYSICS_FROM_COMPOSITIONAL_OR_INFORMATIONAL_STRUCTURE
LAWS_AS_CONSTRAINTS_ON_GLOBAL_STRUCTURE
```

This lane is for overlap mapping, not assimilating PGH to an existing program prematurely.

## Source-quality hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. primary foundational papers or original theorem sources;
2. standard scholarly monographs/textbooks;
3. peer-reviewed surveys or authoritative lecture notes;
4. authoritative reference articles where primary sources are difficult to navigate.

Do not freeze blogs, SEO pages, unsourced summaries, or casual forum posts as scientific sources.

## Selection criteria

A source may enter the frozen landscape only if it materially supports at least one of:

```text
FORMAL_MECHANISM_PRIOR_ART
REPRESENTATION_EQUIVALENCE_PRIOR_ART
COHERENCE_PRIOR_ART
VARIABLE_ARITY_PRIOR_ART
THEORETICAL_EQUIVALENCE_PRIOR_ART
FOUNDATIONAL_OVERLAP
NOVELTY_RISK_ASSESSMENT
TERMINOLOGY_OR_TAXONOMY_CONTROL
```

A source should be rejected or deferred if it is merely keyword-similar, redundant without added authority, inaccessible beyond reliable identification, or too remote from the live PGH questions.

## Coverage and saturation

The operation should seek representative coverage rather than exhaustive bibliography.

Target:

```text
MINIMUM_SEARCH_LANES_WITH_ACCEPTED_COVERAGE = 10
TARGET_FROZEN_SOURCE_RANGE = 24_TO_40
```

The final corpus may be smaller or larger if justified by source quality and redundancy.

Saturation is reached when additional searches primarily return already represented theorem families, standard texts, or terminological variants rather than materially new conceptual lanes.
