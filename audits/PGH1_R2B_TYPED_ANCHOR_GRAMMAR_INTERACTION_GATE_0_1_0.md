## Executive result

The law-free typed anchor does not, while left external to a grammar signature, change the generated formal structure.

Once typed structure is internalized, the internalization matters. But independent review also exposed an important anti-compression correction: in a product doctrine, a single arrow into a product is equivalent data to its full tuple of component arrows. Product-output packaging therefore does **not** evade edge encoding.

The coproduct-output control remains genuinely different. A generic arrow into a coproduct can exist without forcing any arrow to an individual summand.

Thus:

```text
EXTERNAL_TYPING = INERT
PRODUCT_OUTPUT_PACKAGING = COMPONENT_EDGE_INFORMATION_IN_EQUIVALENT_FORM
COPRODUCT_OUTPUT_GENERIC_PROCESS = NONTRIVIAL_BUT_COMPONENT_RESPONSE_UNDERDETERMINING
CANONICAL_INTERNALIZATION = NOT_FOUND
```

The correct new burden is the anchor-to-grammar internalization map.

## T1 — external metadata inertness

Let `T` satisfy `PGH-OBJ-0025`, but keep it outside the grammar signature.

The formal inputs to the free grammar construction are unchanged, so grammar derivability is unchanged:

\[
Free_G(S_{ref};T_{external})=Free_G(S_{ref})
\]

at the relevant formal scope.

This is recorded as:

```text
PGH-DER-0024 = EXTERNAL_TYPING_STRUCTURAL_INERTNESS
```

The empty atomic context-to-record hom result of `PGH-DER-0021` is therefore not repaired merely by carrying typed metadata alongside the grammar.

## T2 — edge internalization

Add

\[
e_{c,r}:X_c\to Y_r
\]

for every typed pair.

Then the typed relation is contained in the hom relation by construction.

No downstream hom-existence result reproducing `T` may be credited as grammar-derived exclusion or bridge structure.

## T3 — product-output control and correction

For one context with fiber

\[
R_c=\{r_0,r_1\},
\]

in `PGH-GRAM-0007`, add

\[
m:X_c\to Y_{r_0}\times Y_{r_1}.
\]

Product projections yield the two component arrows.

However, by the product universal property,

\[
Hom(X,Y_0\times Y_1)
\cong
Hom(X,Y_0)\times Hom(X,Y_1).
\]

So specifying `m` is exactly equivalent formal data to specifying its two component arrows.

The gate therefore assigns:

```text
PRODUCT_OUTPUT_AS_COMPACT_BRIDGE_GENERATION = FAIL
PRODUCT_OUTPUT_AS_COMPONENT_EDGE_PACKAGING = PASS_DIAGNOSIS
```

This correction was made during independent review before integration.

## T4 — coproduct-output control

Use the same typed fiber and same bicartesian doctrine but add

\[
n:X_c\to Y_{r_0}+Y_{r_1}.
\]

Neither individual arrow

\[
X_c\to Y_{r_0}
\]

nor

\[
X_c\to Y_{r_1}
\]

is forced.

For the first, interpret in `Set`:

\[
X_c\mapsto1,
\quad
Y_{r_0}\mapsto\varnothing,
\quad
Y_{r_1}\mapsto1.
\]

Then the coproduct is `1`, so `n` can be interpreted, but no map `1->empty` exists. Swap the record interpretations for the other component.

Therefore a generic process into an aggregate typed output can remain strictly weaker than specifying component edges.

## T6 — context-role representation

The law-free anchor does not determine whether a context token must be represented as an object, process, process source, typed process, index, or another formal role.

That role belongs to the internalization data and cannot be selected retrospectively because it produces a desired bridge.

## Why outcome A does not pass

No accepted result selects one internalization canonically or non-result-directly.

The product control cannot rescue A because it is information-equivalent to a component-edge tuple.

The coproduct control is formally weaker than component edges but is still an additional generic process assumption, not a consequence of `T` plus the doctrine alone.

## Why outcome C does not pass

The coproduct control refutes the claim that **every** nontrivial internalization is equivalent to primitive component-response edges.

It introduces genuine cross-type process structure while leaving individual component arrows underdetermined.

That does not make it physically correct; it only means the admissible bridge family is not exhausted by edge lists.

## Why outcome B passes

External typing is inert and explicit internalizations alter formal consequences. Since the same typed interface and same grammar doctrine do not determine one internalization, the internalization map is a distinct bridge-level burden.
