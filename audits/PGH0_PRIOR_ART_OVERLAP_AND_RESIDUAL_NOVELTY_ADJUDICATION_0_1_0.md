## 7. Residual R1 — Physical Irrelevance Selector

### Statement

A successful strong PGH requires a criterion `C_phys` that determines when two adequate formal presentations differ only representationally rather than physically.

The criterion must not be chosen because it yields a desired algebraic or physical law.

Schematically:

\[
C_{phys}(p,p') \Longrightarrow p \sim_{phys} p'.
\]

The resulting equivalence/forgetful structure may then constrain evaluation or admissibility by coherence.

### Why it survives

The frozen corpus supplies many sophisticated equivalence notions but no universal rule establishing which is physically correct in the abstract PGH setting.

This is not evidence that no such prior art exists elsewhere. It establishes only that the burden is undischarged against the frozen representative landscape.

### Success condition

R1 advances only if a criterion can be stated that is:

```text
NON_RESULT_DIRECTED
REPRESENTATION_ROBUST
NOT_MERELY_EMPIRICAL_EQUIVALENCE_BY_DEFINITION
NOT_A_HIDDEN_PHYSICAL_LAW
CAPABLE_OF_DISCRIMINATING_BETWEEN_COMPETING_EQUIVALENCE_RELATIONS
```

### Failure condition

R1 fails at current program scope if every candidate criterion either:

- presupposes the target physical law;
- depends on a chosen formalism without invariant justification;
- reduces to “whatever transformations preserve the physics,” which is circular;
- or leaves multiple inequivalent selectors without a further principle.

## 8. Residual R2 — Law Exhaustion

### Statement

Even if a physically justified equivalence relation is found, strong PGH still requires that grammatical well-formedness/coherence perform the substantive physical selection.

Using the null-hypothesis notation,

\[
P = \mathrm{Select}_{L}(W(G))
\]

must reduce to a form in which the independent selector `L` does not carry residual physical law.

The strong target is schematically:

\[
P \cong W(G)/{\sim_{phys}}
\]

with no separate `L` doing substantive selection work.

### Why it survives

- Laws-as-constraints shows that law need not be ordinary dynamics, but it does not show that grammar exhausts law.
- Constructor theory centers possible/impossible transformations, but it does not identify all constraint content with a representation-independent generative grammar.
- Operational reconstructions derive theory from principles, but the principles themselves retain substantive physical content.
- MUH identifies physical reality with mathematical structure at a broad level, but does not supply PGH's non-smuggling grammar/exhaustion result.

### Success condition

R2 advances only if a candidate PGH can demonstrate at least one substantive physical exclusion for which no independent law-like premise remains outside the grammar and its independently justified equivalence structure.

### Failure condition

If a separate rule set `L` remains necessary and `L` does the actual physical selection, then strong PGH reduces to a representational wrapper around ordinary law and R2 fails.

### Sequencing status

```text
R2 = LIVE_BUT_NOT_YET_ACTIONABLE
```

R2 should not be attacked before R1 produces a candidate physically justified invariance structure or a different route is independently justified.

## 9. Relationship between R1 and R2

R1 and R2 must not be collapsed.

R1 asks:

> Which distinctions are representation only?

R2 asks:

> After those distinctions are removed, does grammar itself do all substantive physical selection?

One can solve R1 while failing R2. A physically justified equivalence relation could exist while ordinary independent physical laws remain indispensable.

Conversely, claiming law exhaustion without a justified representation quotient risks confusing surface syntax with physical necessity.

Therefore the residual program has an ordered dependency:

\[
R1 \longrightarrow R2.
\]

This arrow is sequencing, not logical proof that R1 is the only possible route to R2.
