## Executive result

A single law-free bridge probe was applied uniformly to all five frozen primitive grammar candidates.

The probe uses only anchor-provided context and record labels as distinct atomic object generators and adds no context-to-record morphism, response relation, probability rule, or empirical partition.

For every candidate doctrine, its free structure on this seed contains no morphism from a distinct atomic context generator `X_c` to a distinct atomic record generator `Y_r`.

Thus the current doctrine-only grammars generate structural organization but no cross-type empirical-response structure under the least target-independent bridge probe.

This is not a prediction that physical responses do not occur. It is a failure of the present grammars to generate the bridge-relevant structure under the locked probe.

## Common bridge probe

For every `c in C` and `r in R` define atomic generators

\[
X_c,\qquad Y_r,
\]

with `X_c` and `Y_r` distinct and with no nonidentity generating arrows between anchor atoms.

For candidate grammar `G_i`, let

\[
F_i=\operatorname{Free}_{G_i}(S_{ref}).
\]

Define only for feasibility analysis

\[
B_i(c,r)\iff \operatorname{Hom}_{F_i}(X_c,Y_r)\neq\varnothing.
\]

The physical interpretation of `B_i` is unestablished.

## T1 — PGH-GRAM-0003 bare monoidal

The free strict monoidal category on atomic object generators and no nonidentity morphism generators has only identity arrows on identical tensor words.

For distinct atomic words:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(X_c,Y_r)=\varnothing.
\]

```text
PGH-GRAM-0003_CROSS_TYPE_RELATION = EMPTY
```

## T2 — PGH-GRAM-0004 symmetric monoidal

Free symmetric structural arrows are generated by identities, tensoring, composition, and symmetry/coherence. They preserve the multiset of atomic generator colors.

Since one atomic context color differs from one atomic record color,

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(X_c,Y_r)=\varnothing.
\]

```text
PGH-GRAM-0004_CROSS_TYPE_RELATION = EMPTY
```

Symmetry can reorder existing factors; it cannot turn one atomic generator into a different atomic generator.

## T3 — PGH-GRAM-0005 cartesian

Suppose the free cartesian structure contained

\[
f:X_c\to Y_r.
\]

By the universal property of the free cartesian category, any assignment of atomic generators into a finite-product category extends to a finite-product-preserving interpretation.

Interpret in `Set` with

\[
X_c\mapsto 1,\qquad Y_r\mapsto\varnothing.
\]

The image of `f` would have to be a function

\[
1\to\varnothing,
\]

which does not exist.

Therefore no such free cartesian morphism exists.

```text
PGH-GRAM-0005_CROSS_TYPE_RELATION = EMPTY
```

## T4 — PGH-GRAM-0006 cocartesian

The analogous separation uses the free finite-coproduct doctrine and the same assignment into `Set`, now regarded as a finite-coproduct category.

```text
PGH-GRAM-0006_CROSS_TYPE_RELATION = EMPTY
```

## T5 — PGH-GRAM-0007 bicartesian

`Set` is bicartesian. An assignment of the distinct atomic generators to `1` and `empty` extends from the free bicartesian structure.

A forced atomic cross-type morphism would again yield the impossible function `1 -> empty`.

Hence:

```text
PGH-GRAM-0007_CROSS_TYPE_RELATION = EMPTY
```

The presence of both products and coproducts does not by itself identify or connect distinct atomic generators.
